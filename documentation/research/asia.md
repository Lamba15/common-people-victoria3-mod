# Asia & Pacific -- Common People Research

## India

### Under East India Company
- Land taxes: 50-60% of total produce (Ryotwari system)
- 1850s scandal: revenue agents discovered torturing peasants to meet demands
- India transformed from exporter of processed goods to raw materials
- Shift to commercial crops (indigo, cotton) worsened food scarcity

### Great Famine (1876-78)
- 6-10 million dead
- Pandita Ramabai documented deaths of her father, mother, and sister
- British relief policies inadequate

### Indigo Revolt (1859-60)
- Planters forced peasants to grow indigo instead of food on best land
- High-interest loans (dadon) kept farmers in perpetual debt, passed to next generation
- Planters burned food crops, seized cattle, beat or imprisoned farmers
- March 1859: ryots in Nadia collectively refused to grow indigo -- first mass nonviolent agricultural strike
- 1860: British passed Indigo Act

### Textile Decline
- India produced 25% of world textiles in 17th century; fell to 2%
- British yarn prices dropped to 10% of 1779 prices by 1812
- Millions of talented weavers reduced to poverty

## China

### Taiping Rebellion (1850-1864)
- 20-30 million dead; mostly poor peasants from Hakka and non-Chinese tribes
- Natural disasters convinced people Dynasty lost Heaven's favor
- Peasants caught between Taiping forces and Qing military

### Opium Addiction
- ~40 million Chinese (10% of population) addicted by end of 19th century
- Ning Lao's husband sold furniture, shirts, and children to sustain addiction
- Sold their 4-year-old daughter to human traffickers; when recovered, sold her again

### Chinese Laborers Abroad
- California: 20,000 in 1852 peak (30% of immigrants)
- Foreign Miners License: $20/month for non-citizens
- Supreme Court ruled Chinese testimony inadmissible against whites
- 1871 Chinese Massacre: 18 lynched by 500+ mob in Los Angeles
- Coolie trade: 500,000 shipped 1847-1874; many kidnapped

### Boxer Rebellion (1900)
- Mostly adolescent peasants from Shandong/Zhili
- Practiced boxing rituals believing they were bullet-proof
- Illiterate, got ideas from traveling operas and sensational stories
- Wanted to drive out foreigners; supported Qing dynasty

## Japan

### Meiji Restoration from Below
- Government abolished samurai class position
- Ex-samurai became civil servants, teachers, merchants, farmers
- Offered yearly stipends converted to one-time government bonds
- Resistance: Saga and Satsuma rebellions

### Women in Silk Mills
- Women: 94-95% of silk workers, 79% of cotton mill workers
- Daughters of poor peasants, initially nearby, later from remote areas
- 13-14 hour days (sometimes 17-18); tuberculosis and severe illness common
- Unilateral labor contracts; factories advanced travel funds, creating indenture
- Exception: Tomioka Mill -- 8-hour days, Sunday holidays, elementary school access

## Southeast Asia

### Dutch East Indies (Indonesia) -- Cultivation System (1830-1870)
- Javanese peasants forced to cultivate cash crops on government land
- Villages required to devote 1/5 to 1/3 of arable land to government crops
- Worked without compensation or at far below market value
- 1849-1850 famine: millions affected in Central Java
- Max Havelaar (1860 novel by Multatuli) exposed the system; became international sensation

### Philippines
- Spanish hacienda system with encomienda labor
- Jose Rizal's novels (Noli Me Tangere, El Filibusterismo) exposed colonial exploitation
- Katipunan revolution (1896): Andres Bonifacio led secret society of workers and peasants
- Under American occupation (1898+): "benevolent assimilation" masked continued exploitation

### Vietnam (French Indochina)
- Forced labor on rubber plantations
- Colonial rice export policies caused food insecurity for Vietnamese peasants

### Siam (Thailand)
- Only Southeast Asian country not colonized
- Slavery existed; gradually abolished by Chulalongkorn (completed 1905)
- Corvee labor of 3 months/year for commoners

## Australia
- Convict labor: 162,000 transported (1788-1868)
- Aboriginal experience: dispossession, frontier violence, forced labor
- 1851 Gold Rush: Chinese miners faced Foreign Miners' Licence and violence

## New Zealand
- Treaty of Waitangi (1840): Maori ceded sovereignty (or did they? -- translation disputes)
- New Zealand Wars (1845-1872): Maori land confiscation
- Maori population declined from ~80,000 (1840) to ~42,000 (1896)
