# Egypt -- Common People Research

## The Corvee System and Fellahin Life

- Fellahin were forced into corvee labor gangs for public works, irrigation, canal construction
- By 1865-1868, the government imposed a 70% increase in land taxes
- By early 1870s, one-third of rural Egypt was completely landless
- Corvee officially abolished after 1882 British takeover (but enforcement was slow)
- Muhammad Ali dictated annually what and how much peasants could grow
- Government monopolized purchase and sale of peasant goods -- the farmer had NO market freedom
- Purchasing power of Cairo wage laborers DECLINED from 1800 to 1907

## Muhammad Ali's Reforms (1805-1849)

- Introduced cotton as a forced cash crop
- Subjected peasants to state supervision over their holdings
- Built 30 cotton mills by early 1830s employing 30,000 workers
- Created state-owned arsenals, iron foundries, textile mills
- Egypt had world's 5th most productive cotton industry per capita in early 1800s
- Historian Zachary Lockman: Egypt could have matched Japan or USA for "autonomous capitalist development"
- Jean Batou: Economic conditions for rapid industrialization existed in 1820s-1830s
- Industry collapsed after Muhammad Ali's death (1849); Egypt became colonial raw material supplier

## Conscription Horror

- 1822 conscription enrolled 130,000 men, mostly fellahin
- 1824 revolt in the south showed how hated military service was
- Peasants fled villages to avoid conscription, sometimes as far as Syria
- **Men ripped out their own front teeth to avoid service** -- strong front teeth were needed to reload rifles
- Wives and families no longer allowed to follow armies -- families torn apart by policy
- Crimean War: Egypt sent 40,000 soldiers. One ship (Miftah-i cihad) sank with ~1,000 aboard; only 130 survived

## The Cotton Boom and Bust (1861-1865)

### The Boom
- American Civil War cut off 80% of British cotton supply
- Price per kantar: $12 (1859) -> $45 (1864) -- nearly 4x increase
- Egyptian exports: 600,000 cantars (1861) -> 1.3 million (1863) -- doubled in 2 years
- Egypt "ceased nearly all agricultural activity unrelated to cotton"
- Cotton became 93% of Egypt's export revenue

### A Moderate Farmer's Experience (5-10 feddans, circa 1863)
- Spring: plant cotton instead of wheat like grandfather
- Summer: constant watering, weeding -- cotton is labor-intensive
- Harvest: pick cotton frantically before price drops
- Suddenly: wife buys new dress, children get shoes, family eats meat occasionally
- Dreams of buying more land. Assumes prices stay high forever
- Borrows against next harvest

### A Poor Farmer's Experience (1-2 feddans)
- Barely survived on wheat; cotton means slightly better food
- But local food prices SPIKE because everyone planted cotton instead of food
- Vegetables become luxury, grain prices rise
- Still barely surviving, now dependent on volatile global price

### The Crash (1865)
- American Civil War ends
- British traders immediately abandon Egyptian cotton for cheaper American supply
- Cotton price CRASHES
- Farmers holding cotton at harvest expecting $45/kantar get $15-20 instead
- Government imposes 70% land tax INCREASE (1865-1868) to cover state debt
- Result: 3,000,000 feddans lost to peasants by 1868
- By 1870s: one-third of rural Egypt completely landless

### Who Benefited
- Village headmen (sheikhs) owning 20-50 feddans: TRIPLED wealth
- Bought slaves to work expanded cotton fields
- Rural slave population TRIPLED: 39,762 (1848) -> 144,592 (1868)

## The Suez Canal (1859-1869) -- Forced Labor

### Recruitment
- Khedive Said Pasha used forced corvee labor
- ~10,000 workers/month in early years; reached 25,000/month after 1861
- Total mobilized: estimated 20,000-120,000+ workers over 10 years
- Conscripted from villages throughout North and South Egypt

### The Filtering at Al-Zaqaziq
- Weak men rejected (returned to villages)
- Strong men separated out
- Tied together with ropes
- Marched 4 days on foot to construction site
- Provisions: one bottle of water per day, one piece of dry bread
- No rest; men died during the march

### On-Site Conditions
- Hand-digging in desert heat (shovels, baskets)
- 12-16 hour days, no safety equipment
- Insufficient food, contaminated water, open sewers
- European banks/bakeries/bars on one side; Egyptian worker misery on the other
- Deaths by disease: cholera, dysentery, smallpox, tuberculosis, hepatitis
- In 1865: so many corpses not enough men to bury them
- Estimated deaths: 20,000 to 120,000

### The 1863 Ban on Corvee
- Ismail Pasha refused to continue corvee labor supply
- Company hired free laborers (Greeks, Italians, Dalmatians) and brought steam-powered machinery
- Conditions improved slightly with free labor

## Slavery in 19th Century Egypt

### Scale and Types
- Estimated 30,000-150,000 slaves at any given time
- Male slaves: soldiers, bureaucrats, field laborers (especially after cotton boom)
- Female slaves: domestic servants, harem concubines (sexual slavery), field laborers
- Eunuchs: household management, harem guards
- Sources: Caucasus, Europe (white slaves), Sudan and Sub-Saharan Africa (black slaves)

### The Cotton Boom Slavery Explosion (1848-1868)
- Rural slave population TRIPLED: 39,762 -> 144,592
- Village headmen bought enslaved workers for cotton field labor
- Predominantly young males (ages 15-40) indicating field labor purpose

### Abolition (1877)
- Anglo-Egyptian Slave Trade Convention banned slave TRADE (not slavery itself)
- Achieved through British pressure, not domestic Egyptian movement
- Slavery gradually phased out; didn't fully die until ~1930s
- After abolition: agricultural wages ROSE as demand for wage laborers increased
- Sudanese population in Egypt declined: 149,312 (1882) -> 24,766 (1917)

## Egyptian Women (19th Century)

### Rural Women
- Worked alongside men in fields
- Spun yarn at home (specialized female task)
- Village midwives
- No property rights; could be forced into marriage by guardians
- Illiterate (except rare exceptions)
- Limited to family sphere

### Urban Working-Class Women
- Weavers, street vendors, midwives, washerwomen, domestic servants
- By late 19th century: pushed OUT of skilled trades into casual service work
- Those in "improper" occupations (dancing, coffeehouse serving) monitored by police, arrested, forcibly relocated
- Lower wages than men (but participated in strikes by 1900s)

### Feminist Movement
- **Qasim Amin** (1863-1908): "The Liberation of Women" (1899). Argued women's liberation was prerequisite for Egypt's liberation from foreign domination. Sparked "frenzied public debate"
- **Huda Sha'arawi** (1879-1947): Egypt's first feminist
  - 1909: Created first philanthropic society run by Egyptian women
  - 1914: Founded Intellectual Association of Egyptian Women
  - 1919: Organized women-led demonstrations against British occupation (150-300 upper-class women marched)
  - 1923: Founded Egyptian Feminist Union; iconic veil removal at Cairo train station
- **Bahithat al-Badiya** (Malak Hifni Nasif, 1886-1918): Gave landmark lecture at Egyptian University 1909; blended feminism + nationalism + moderate Islam
- **Zainab al-Fawwaz** (1860-1914): Pioneer journalist; documented 456 women's lives and achievements

## Rifa'a al-Tahtawi (1801-1873) -- The Enlightenment Reformer

### Biography
- Born in Tahta, Sohag (Upper Egypt)
- Al-Azhar graduate, sent to Paris 1826-1831 as imam with Egyptian educational mission
- Studied ethics, philosophy, mathematics; read Voltaire, Rousseau, Montesquieu
- Founded School of Languages (Madrasat al-Alsun) in Cairo, 1835

### Key Ideas
- Education (especially women's education) as foundation of societal change
- European science does NOT contradict Islam -- integration possible
- Women's chastity results from proper education, not seclusion
- Government should be based on reason and justice
- Parliamentary systems compatible with Islamic values

### Major Works
- "Takhlis al-Ibriz fi Talkhis Bariz" (The Quintessence of Paris, 1834): documented 5 years in Paris, one of earliest Muslim accounts of Enlightenment Europe
- "A Guide to Education of Girls and Boys" (1872): called for coeducational textbooks
- Supervised translation of 2,000+ foreign works into Arabic

### Alt-History: What If Tahtawi Had Full Power?
- Egypt develops own industrial capacity instead of failing mills under Muhammad Ali
- Nationalist intellectual movement 30 years earlier
- Women enter education/professions 50+ years before they actually did
- Egypt might resist British occupation through intellectual/political unity
- Peasant education (agricultural science, literacy) instead of just extraction
- Consumer industries for domestic markets instead of just cotton for export

## Egyptian Labor History

### The Cigarette Workers' Strike (1899-1900)
- December 1899 - February 1900: Greek and Egyptian cigarette workers struck in Cairo
- Factory owners introduced machinery, fired large numbers, cut wages
- Working hours: 17 hours/day
- Led to formation of Egypt's FIRST workers' union (1899)
- Mixed Association of Cigarette Workers united foreign and Egyptian workers

### Other Early Strikes
- Tramway workers (Cairo)
- Railroad workers
- Port workers (Alexandria)
- Cairo Water Company workers

### Manual Trades Workers' Union (1908)
- Founded under Egyptian Nationalist Party umbrella
- Purpose: build national working-class support base

### Alt-History: Egyptian Workers' Revolution (1880s)
- Egypt had ALL conditions for workers' revolution BEFORE Russia
- Tens of thousands of industrial workers in Cairo by 1880s
- Corvee system creating mass resentment
- British occupation exploiting workers for profit
- If labor unified with nationalists in 1880s instead of 1919: could have expelled British 25+ years earlier

## The Urabi Revolt (1882)

- Emerged from economic hardship among peasant-farmers
- Anger at taxes going to wealthy Europeans and Turks
- Common people regarded Ahmad Urabi as hero despite state media calling him traitor
- Riots: homes looted, parts of Cairo burned, ~50 Europeans and ~250 Egyptians killed
- Laid foundation for mass politics in Egypt

## The Khedival Period

### Khedive Ismail (1863-1879): The Magnificent Spender
- Opera House (1869): first in Africa/Arab world, 850 seats, Verdi's Aida premiered 1871
- Railways, boulevards, parks, gas lighting (Paris-inspired New Cairo)
- Debt: £7 million (1863) -> £100 million (1876) -- 14-fold increase
- 1876 bankruptcy -> Caisse de la Dette (international debt commission with British, French, Austrian, Italian, Russian seats)

### Daily Life Contrast
- Cairo: European-style quarters, gas lights, paved streets, opera, French restaurants
- Upper Egypt villages: UNCHANGED -- fellahin working for subsistence, crushing taxes, corvee

## British Occupation (1882-1914)

- "Veiled protectorate" -- British Consul-General had ultimate power
- Lord Cromer (1883-1907): laissez-faire policies benefiting British investors
- Cotton production for British mills -- enriched landowners, impoverished peasants
- 13-hour days in cotton gins, 17-hour days in some sectors
- Modernization was real (railroads, schools, hospitals) BUT profits went to Cairo businessmen and London investors

## Muhammad Abduh and Islamic Modernism (1849-1905)

- Led movement to revitalize Islam for the modern world
- Three-part program: free Islamic thought from blind imitation, reform Arabic literature, increase political participation
- At al-Azhar: introduced curriculum combining religion with science, math, philosophy
- Replaced memorization with critical thinking
- Rejected literal Quranic interpretation in favor of equity and common sense

## Event Ideas

- Tooth extraction to avoid conscription -- a man rips out his teeth, his wife watches
- Cotton boom: Layla's harvest worth triple. She buys a dress. Next year the price collapses.
- Suez Canal conscription: husband taken to dig. Conditions lethal. Does he come back?
- British occupation: foreign official inspects the village. Doesn't speak Arabic. Writes in a ledger.
- Urabi revolt: the village takes sides. Neighbor joins rebels. The British come.
- Veil removal: young woman in Cairo removes veil after hearing about Sha'arawi. Family reacts.
- Al-Azhar reform: village imam returns from Cairo with new ideas. Old imam is furious.
- Opera house opening: Khedive hosts Europeans while 100 miles south, canal workers die burying previous workers.
- The cigarette strike: Fatima rolls cigarettes 14 hours/day for 2 piastres. Factory introduces machinery. She joins strike. First time she realizes she has power.
- The slave-buying sheikh: village headman buys 3 enslaved men during cotton boom. When slavery abolished, pivots to starvation wages.
