# Europe -- Common People Research

## France

### Paris 1848 Revolution -- June Days
- 40,000-50,000 workers built barricades across Paris over 5 days
- Trigger: plans to close National Workshops (minimal income for unemployed)
- General Cavaignac ordered brutal crackdown with heavy casualties

### Paris Commune (1871) -- Women
- **Louise Michel** (1830-1905): led 10,000 women in combat during "Week of Blood"
- 62,000 of 114,000 industrial jobs held by women in 1871 Paris
- Women organized field hospitals, vigilance committees, city workshops with equal pay
- 10,000-15,000 Communards killed in suppression

### Lyon Silk Workers (Canuts) Revolts
- 1831: workers' strike notice: "all looms will stop until further notice on Monday 21st"
- Employers paying 6-7 francs/day but government said "too much"
- Workers destroyed machines and account books
- Targeted both industrialists AND bankers

### Zola's Documentation
- **Germinal** (1885): Zola went underground in Anzin/Denain mines to witness conditions firsthand
- **L'Assommoir** (1877): working-class Paris, poverty, alcoholism

### Phylloxera Wine Crisis (1860s-1890s)
- Destroyed most French vineyards; output fell two-thirds (1875-1889)
- Women paid 1.5-1.75 francs/day for pest removal; men paid 3 francs for same work

## Germany

### Silesian Weavers' Uprising (1844)
- Hand-loom weavers starving from industrial competition
- Destroyed machines, account books, title deeds
- Contemporary newspaper: "No windows, only debris of window pane... rubble wherever our foot turns"
- Heinrich Heine's poem "The Silesian Weavers" published in Marx's newspaper, 50,000 copies as leaflet

### Ruhr Miners
- By 1900: most heavily industrialized region of Europe
- 1889: nearly 100,000 miners struck demanding 20-25% wage increase, 8-hour day
- Employers forced to settlement but didn't honor terms

### Bismarck's Social Insurance (1883-1889)
- 1883 Health Insurance: medical treatment, medicines, childbirth support (26 weeks), death benefits
- Unintended consequence: worker-elected health bureau committees gave Social Democrats their first foothold in public administration
- Workers thought it "didn't go far enough" but took the democratic access

### Hamburg Cholera Epidemic (1892)
- 8,594-10,000 deaths in 2 months
- City run by merchant families prioritizing trade over welfare
- Poor disproportionately affected; wealth provided protection
- One year later: citizens voted out merchants, replaced with Social Democrats

## Italy

### Southern Poverty and Mass Emigration
- 59 of 60 families didn't own enough land for burial
- After 1871 unification: South saw NO economic benefit, taxes increased
- Annual emigration averaged 220,000 (1876-1900)
- Cholera epidemics, malaria, vine parasites combined into humanitarian crisis

### Risorgimento from Peasant View
- Garibaldi's soldiers found Sicilian peasants barefoot, in goatskins, kissing hands
- Peasants had no knowledge of "Italy" as concept
- Brigands War caused more bloodshed than all Italian wars of independence

### Sicilian Fasci (1893-1894)
- 170+ Fasci, 300,000+ members
- Patti Corleone (July 30, 1893): peasants drafted Italy's first agrarian contract
- Brutally suppressed

### Sicilian Sulfur Mines -- Carusi (Child Slavery)
- Children aged 4-7+ indentured to miners via "soccorso morto" (dead aid) loans
- Debt perpetual, neither parent nor child could repay -- slavery in all but name
- Worked 8-10 hours underground; loads: 25-80 kg depending on age
- Temperature: 45C with 100% humidity; naked or only leather strap
- Endemic sexual abuse; no legal protections
- Mines operated until 1970s-1980s

## Ireland -- The Great Famine (1845-1852)

- Population: 8.4 million (1844) -> 6.6 million (1851)
- ~1 million dead from starvation/disease; ~2 million emigrated
- Workhouse deaths: 14,662 (1846) -> 68,890 (1848) -- 4.7x increase in 2 years
- Families separated in workhouses by age/gender
- Eviction rate rose ~1000% during crisis
- Coffin ships: crowded, disease-ridden; Mary Mulvihill (1847): "packed like cattle with foul water and worm-riddled food"
- Soup kitchens sustained 3 million daily at peak (1847)

## Spain

### Barcelona Tragic Week (1909)
- Strikes over wage cuts and military conscription (Rif War)
- Street fighting, 80+ religious buildings burned
- 75-100 deaths; Francisco Ferrer y Guardia (education advocate) executed
- International protests against executions

### Andalusian Latifundia
- Vast estates from 13th century; peasants worked with minimal protection
- 16-hour days, seasonal employment, wages insufficient for unemployed months
- "Cortijo" farmhouses only temporarily inhabited by day laborers during harvest

## Austria-Hungary
- By 1900: nationalisms mobilized middle classes and increasing numbers of workers
- Language became crucial: German speakers lost Bohemian Diet majority (1880)
- Landless peasants: 25% of population (1900)
- WWI: rationing (1915) sparked food riots; gendarmes requisitioned food, sometimes seed grain

## Scandinavia
- Population tripled in 19th century; agricultural limits in Norway
- Great Swedish Famine (1867-1869): 60,000 emigrated
- Fishing communities: men away for months, women ran households alone
- "Prairie madness" among Scandinavian emigrants in American plains

## Poland (Under Partition)
- Russian partition: Russification, restrictions on Polish language, anti-Catholic campaigns
- Prussian partition: gradual enfranchisement, more progressive
- Austrian partition: most lenient, serfdom abolished 1848
- 3.6 million emigrants from Polish lands (1870-1914)

## Greece (After 1832)
- "Law for Dotation of Greek Families" (1835): 2,000 drachmas credit, 12-acre farms
- By 1870: most families owned ~20 acres
- Remained very poor; only significant exports: currants, raisins, tobacco
- Brigandage persistent security problem

## Balkans
- Ottoman rule: rise of commercially-oriented estates (ciftliks) tied peasants to land
- National awakening first noticeable among peasant populations
- Independence through Russo-Turkish War (1877-78), Balkan Wars (1912-13)
