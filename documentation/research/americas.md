# Americas -- Common People Research

## Mexico

### Hacienda Peon Life
- Over 95% of peons carried debt by 1910; many accumulated MORE debt despite working
- Over 95% of communal villages lost their lands by 1910 (Porfirio Diaz era)
- Real wages DECREASED during 1876-1910 despite modernization
- Yucatan: Maya workers accumulated large debts at marriage, never repaid; slavery-like

### Caste War of Yucatan (1847-1901)
- Maya rebels controlled three-fourths of Yucatan Peninsula at peak (June-July 1848)
- One of most successful modern Native American revolts
- Peasants demanded reduction of Indigenous taxes to one real/month

### Mexican Revolution (1910) -- Peasant Perspective
- Emiliano Zapata: from village of Anenecuilco, modest mestizo peasant family
- Plan de Ayala: immediate restitution of village lands taken during Diaz era
- "Tierra y Libertad" (Land and Liberty); "Better to die on your feet than live on your knees"
- 25,000 peasant soldiers in Liberation Army of the South
- When occupying Mexico City: went door-to-door humbly asking for food -- notable restraint

### Soldaderas (Women Fighters)
- Cooked, mended, foraged, tended wounded, buried dead
- Carried heavy burdens: food, cookware, bedding, often children
- Spies, messengers, nurses, smugglers; some became commanders (coronelas)
- Villa's Division of the North (1914): 1,256 soldaderas and 554 children with 4,557 male soldiers

## Brazil

### Coffee Plantation Colonos (1870-1930)
- Italian families tended 5,000-8,000 coffee trees
- Physically exhausting year-round: clearing forest, planting, weeding, harvesting, processing
- Prinetti Decree (1902): Italian government banned subsidized immigration to Brazil due to exploitation reports
- Japanese immigrants (1908+): 781 on first ship; many escaped at night (yonige), suicide rates indicate desperation

### Canudos War (1896-1897)
- Religious community in Bahia interior led by Conselheiro
- Mostly rural peasants fleeing drought, debt, land scarcity
- No social differences, communal ownership of herds and crops
- Media portrayed them as monarchist conspirators; reality: "desperately poor peasants"
- Women outnumbered men 2:1 toward end

### Cangaceiros (Northeast Bandits)
- "Social bandits" -- behaved well to poor, targeted wealthy
- Gave free parties, bought goods at above-market prices from shopkeepers
- Lampiao (Virgulino Ferreira da Silva) most famous leader
- Violence against women documented; sexual assault, domestic abuse

### Favelas Origins
- After 1888 abolition: freed slaves with no housing, no employment
- Settled on steep slopes unsuitable for commercial development
- Also: Canudos War veterans settling on Rio hillside awaiting government payment
- No sewage, electricity, water, or police protection for decades

### Slavery in Brazil (Final Years)
- Last country in Americas to abolish (May 13, 1888)
- 1887-88: plantation exodus reached crisis; thousands fleeing daily
- Lei Aurea freed 700,000 remaining enslaved persons
- Post-abolition: NO integration policies, education, land access, or employment help

## Argentina

### Immigration Boom (1880-1930)
- ~6 million immigrants; Buenos Aires: 300,000 (1880) -> 1.5 million (1914)
- Italians over 2 million (largest group, ~50%, many from Genoa)

### Conventillos (Tenements)
- Long houses with small rooms around central patio -- birthplace of tango
- 1904: 138,188 people in 43,873 rooms in 2,462 tenement houses (10%+ of population)
- Rents tripled from 1870 to 1907

### Tenants' Strike (1907)
- ~3 months; 32,000 workers on strike over rent increases
- Over 100 tenant organizers

### Tragic Week (1919)
- 2,500 metalworkers striking for wages, union recognition, 8-hour day
- Anarchist FORA expanded to general strike
- Police, military, and Argentine Patriotic League crushed uprising
- Hundreds dead, mostly workers

## Chile -- Santa Maria School Massacre (1907)
- Nitrate miners converged on Iquique demanding better conditions
- "18 Pence Strike" -- the wage demanded by day laborers
- Strikers camped at school for a week; given 1 hour to disband
- General Renard ordered troops to fire: rifle and machine gun
- Over 2,000 victims -- men, women, children
- Official suppression of knowledge for decades

## Peru

### Guano Workers (Chinese Coolies)
- 1849-1874: ~100,000 men kidnapped or lured from taverns, shackled on ships
- Ships designed for 300 carried 600+; each man allotted less than 8 sq ft
- Housed in former slave barracks; uric acid in guano dust infected lungs
- By 1860: believed not one of 4,000 Chinese sent had survived -- "social murder"
- Contract extensions via fabricated debts prolonged indenture into 1890s

### Putumayo Rubber Atrocities
- Julio Cesar Arana enslaved indigenous population; 100,000+ died
- Population: 50,000 (1890) -> 6,000 (1920)
- Punishments for not meeting quotas: flagellation, dismemberment, execution
- Roger Casement investigated: 30,000 killed in 12 years

## Haiti
- World's first Black republic from successful slave revolution
- Freed slaves refused to work plantations; became peasant cultivators
- France demanded $21 billion (today's value) in reparations from the people they enslaved
- US occupation (1915-1934): forced corvee labor, men seized on roads, bound in chain gangs
- Widely believed to be return to slavery; 15,000-40,000 killed

## Cuba
- Sugar dominated; 90,000 slaves imported 1856-1860
- Ten Years' War (1868-78): 250,000 lives; deep schism over slavery among revolutionaries

## Canada
### Residential Schools (1831-1970)
- 150,000+ children attended; 7,000+ died
- Children numbered, hair cut; beaten for speaking native languages
- "She'd lift up her hands and bring the stick down on me. I've still got bumps and scars"

### Chinese Railroad Workers (1880-1885)
- 17,000 employed; at least 600 died during construction
- Most dangerous tasks given to Chinese; handled nitroglycerin for blasting
- Paid $1/day -- half the pay of white workers
- Families in China often never notified of deaths
