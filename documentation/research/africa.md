# Africa -- Common People Research

## West Africa

### Sokoto Caliphate (Northern Nigeria)
- Common people depended on "great houses" (aristocratic compounds) for food and clothing
- Slavery pervasive -- at times over 50% of population
- Major crops: cotton, indigo, kola nuts, shea nuts, grain, rice, tobacco, onions
- Exchange medium: cowry shells, gold, enslaved people
- Sharia law governed inheritance, marriage, criminal justice

### Yoruba Kingdoms
- Complex urban civilization; Ibadan had 100,000+ inhabitants by mid-19th century
- Warfare between kingdoms (1830s-1890s) displaced populations
- Women dominated local markets and trade

### Ashanti Empire
- Sophisticated bureaucracy and military; gold trade wealth
- Queen Mother (Asantehemaa) held significant political power
- 1900: Queen Yaa Asantewaa led rebellion defending the Golden Stool

### Dahomey and the Amazons
- All-female military regiment (Mino/Ahosi): 1,000-6,000 warriors
- Recruited from captives, volunteers, and enrolled women
- Trained rigorously; fought with dedication
- Dissolved after French conquest (1894)

## East Africa

### Zanzibar Clove Plantations
- Clove production dominated by Arab/Omani landowners using enslaved African labor
- Estimated 60,000-100,000 enslaved people on Zanzibar by 1860s
- Abolished gradually after British protectorate (1890)

### German East Africa -- Maji Maji Rebellion (1905-1907)
- African resistance to forced cotton cultivation and hut tax
- Warriors believed sacred water (maji) would turn German bullets to water
- Germans responded with scorched-earth campaign: burned crops and villages
- Estimated 75,000-300,000 African deaths, mostly from famine
- One of deadliest colonial conflicts in African history

## Southern Africa

### Zulu Kingdom
- Elaborate social structure: king/aristocracy, free commoners, enslaved people
- Military conscription (amabutho): men segregated for training
- Women managed farms/households while men at war
- Annual first-fruit ceremony: food, music, regiment naming

### Battle of Isandlwana (1879)
- Zulu warriors defeated British army -- one of Britain's worst colonial defeats
- 1,300+ British and colonial troops killed
- Proved African armies could defeat European forces in open battle

### Diamond/Gold Mining (Kimberley, Witwatersrand)
- Compound system: workers confined to enclosed compounds during contracts
- Migrant labor system: men left families for months/years
- Dangerous work: tunnel collapses, silicosis (lung disease from rock dust)
- Workers from across southern Africa; diverse languages in compounds

### Battle of Adwa (1896) -- Ethiopia
- Emperor Menelik II united provincial rulers against Italian invasion
- 80,000-120,000 troops mobilized from nearly all Ethiopian regions
- Decisive Ethiopian victory: 6,000+ Italian troops killed, 3,800 captured
- Only African state to defeat European colonial power in open battle
- Became rallying point for Pan-African movement worldwide

## Congo Free State -- The Rubber Terror

### Leopold II's System
- Personal property of Belgian king (not Belgian state)
- Indigenous people forced to collect rubber under quota system

### Hand-Cutting
- Officers ordered soldiers to provide one victim's hand per bullet spent
- Hands collected as trophies to prove bullets weren't "wasted"
- October 10, 1896 (Bandakea Wijiko village): 50 killed, all right hands cut off

### Specific Atrocities
- Officers ordered: heads cut off, hung on village palisades
- Women and children hung in cross formations
- 1,600 workers found by Casement: badly beaten, whipped, starved
- Estimated 30,000 killed in 12 years by one company alone

### Investigators
- Roger Casement (British consul): investigated 1910, published findings
- Arthur Conan Doyle: publicized the horror
- E.D. Morel: led international campaign

### Survivor Testimonies
- 1950s: Belgian missionaries interviewed survivors, transcribed oral histories
- Alice Seeley Harris: 1904 photograph of Nsala, whose hand was cut off -- became iconic image
- Population estimates suggest millions died during Leopold's rule (1885-1908)

## Ethiopia
- Life under Emperor Menelik: sophisticated court, diverse peoples
- Coffee cultivation: Ethiopia as birthplace of coffee
- Slavery existed in Ethiopia; abolished gradually in 20th century
- After Adwa: Ethiopia became symbol of African independence and resistance

## Event Ideas

- Zulu warrior conscripted into amabutho: leaves family, woman runs farm alone
- Diamond miner in compound: misses family, dangerous work, diverse languages
- Maji Maji rebellion: a farmer believes the sacred water will protect him. It doesn't.
- Congo rubber quota: a village cannot meet the quota. The Force Publique arrives.
- Ashanti woman trader: dominant in market, watches British erode her world
- Ethiopian soldier at Adwa: pride of defeating Europeans, told to his grandchildren for generations
- Zanzibar slave: works clove plantation, hears rumors of abolition. What changes?
