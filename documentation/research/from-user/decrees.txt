Decrees
Jump to navigationJump to search
Please help with verifying or updating older sections of this article.
At least some were last verified for version 1.10.

Decrees are effects that can be applied to individual states to improve them in many ways. Multiple decrees can be used simultaneously on the same state, with the exception of the three decrees encouraging certain industries which are mutually exclusive. Nearly all decrees require a certain technology to have been researched in order to be used. Decrees can be applied either by right-clicking on a province or through the political lens.

Decrees cost a base of Hud authority.png 100 authority each to be applied, except Emergency Relief Emergency Relief which costs 50. A Role ruler.png ruler with the character traits Trait ambitious.png Ambitious and Trait imperious.png Imperious reduces the cost of decrees by −25% and −20% respectively. Certain events can also reduce the cost. Decree cost reductions are capped at a minimum 10% of the base cost, even if modifiers would reduce the cost more.

As well as their immediate effects, decrees also enables certain events.

Strategic considerations
Ambox outdated info.png
Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.10.
Decrees can be a powerful tool, especially for countries with fewer states to punch above their weight. As an example, Flag of Belgium Belgium can enact three or four decrees in each of its two starting states at the game start. Apart from decrees, the other primary use of authority is to raise consumption taxes. The surplus bonus is faster law enactment, which can be very useful when implementing a new law, especially an unpopular one. But when a law is not being implemented, a surplus has no benefit, so one should generally use all available authority in that case.

The amount of available authority tends to decrease over the course of the game for most countries due to increasing liberalization. So if the state budget is very dependent on decrees and consumption taxes, there should be a plan for what to do, when liberalizing. Nevertheless, it is generally the case that modern laws are worth a lot more than potential decrees.

When considering to enact a decree there are two important factors:

The value of the decree compared to introducing an additional consumption tax
The value of the decree compared to liberalizing further
Decrees are more powerful when the country has around five or fewer states, while consumption taxes are better in larger countries.

Road maintenance and construction
The Road Maintenance decree is not efficient to maintain State status infrastructure.png infrastructure outside of emergencies and temporary solutions; railroads and ports should be built instead as the long term solution. However, using the decree to boost construction can be quite effective; particularly early in the game, when countries do not have a much construction available and thus are constructing in only one or two states at any given time.
