# Middle East & Central Asia -- Common People Research

## Persia/Iran (Qajar Dynasty)

### Peasant Life
- Systematic exploitation by courtiers, officials, tribal leaders, landlords
- Large landowners lived in cities, employed overseers to extract maximum surplus
- European influence: Iran became source of cheap raw materials (opium, rice, tobacco)
- Government sold state lands to private owners, disrupting traditional tenure

### The Tobacco Protest (1891-92) -- First Mass Uprising
- Grand Ayatollah Mirza Shirazi: fatwa stating tobacco use = "war against the Imam of the Age"
- Hajji Mohammad Kazem (king of merchants) organized bazaar opposition across Tehran, Shiraz, Mashhad, Isfahan
- Even Shah's wives stopped smoking publicly
- Government forced to cancel British monopoly -- rare victory against European economic domination

### Constitutional Revolution (1905-1911)
- Caspian region: over 3,000 fishermen of Anzali protested after Lianozov Fisheries monopoly forced them to surrender catches at low fixed rates
- Peasant-turned-workers spread urban revolutionary ideas to villages
- Janet Afary's scholarship reveals crucial roles of neglected groups

### Nomadic Tribes (Qashqa'i, Bakhtiari)
- 350-mile seasonal migrations between winter/summer pastures (1-3 months each)
- Mixed economy: sheep/goat herding, grain, women's carpet weaving (internationally famous)
- State promoted sedentarization programs; Qajar shahs relied on tribal khans for tax collection

### Women's Worlds in Qajar Iran
- qajarwomen.org: ~3,000 digitized primary sources (letters, poetry, photographs, legal documents)
- Unpublished poetry and essays by women, marriage contracts, social critiques

## Ottoman Heartland (Anatolia)

### Peasant Life and Tanzimat
- Family tax status (cift-hane unit) based on land, labor capacity, surveys every 20-30 years
- 1858 Land Code: transformed usufruct rights to ownership -- but low registration caused land grabs by elites
- "Local merchants or city magnates often filed whole villages in their own names"

### Ottoman Workers
- After 1838 Anglo-Turkish Convention: cheap British imports forced wage depression
- Production shifted to rural areas using women's and children's labor
- Coal mining at Zonguldak: brutal conditions (1822-1920)

### WWI Conscription
- Men aged 20-45 conscripted; fields left untended
- ~500,000 deserters driven by poor conditions and lost hope
- "Many peasant soldiers were absolutely alien to basics of military life"

### Armenian Genocide (1915-16)
- 664,000-1.2 million killed through massacres, deportation, starvation
- 800,000-1.2 million deported; only ~200,000 alive by late 1916
- Eyewitness: Ambassador Morgenthau: "a carefully planned scheme to thoroughly extinguish the Armenian race"
- USC Shoah Foundation: 1,388+ survivor interviews

## Syria/Lebanon

### Tanyus Shahin -- Peasant Leader (1815-1895)
- Maronite muleteer who led Mount Lebanon peasant uprising (1858-1860)
- Peasants rebelled against heavy taxes and feudal privileges
- 1859: drove out Maronite Khazen nobility, declared "peasants' republic"
- Plundered silk/wheat warehouses and redistributed among peasants
- 500-600 Khazen family members fled impoverished to Beirut

### Druze-Maronite Massacres (1860-61)
- ~20,000 Christians killed by Druzes
- 380 Christian villages and 560 churches destroyed
- Sectarian violence replaced class conflict

### Silk Industry
- Major Lebanese export; Maronite peasants became prosperous through silk cultivation
- Some peasants became moneylenders to Druze landlords -- power inversion

## Arabia

### Bedouin Traditional Life
- Black goat-hair tents divided by curtain into male, family, cooking sections
- Traveled in groups of 50-100; each tribe held reserved livestock areas with exclusive wells
- Pastoral nomadism with raiding supplementing income

### Ibn Saud's Ikhwan Movement (1912-1929)
- Bedouin settled in colonies (hijrahs) around desert oases
- Each offered: living quarters, mosques, schools, agricultural equipment, arms
- Enforced: communal prayer, tithing (zakat), prohibition on smoking, music, feuds
- 1926-1929: Ikhwan revolted against Ibn Saud for introducing telephones, automobiles, telegraphs
- Crushed at Battle of Sibila (March 1929)

## Yemen
- Mocha: world's sole coffee exporter until early 1700s; chief port with English, Dutch, French trading companies
- 3,000-4,000 Banyan merchants dominated trade by 1730s
- Population declined: ~20,000 (early 1800s) -> 1,000 (1930s)
- British moved base to Aden (1839), diverting Yemen's trade

## Palestine
- Fellahin: ~70% of population; most lived under Ottoman administration
- "Serfs working properties owned by wealthy Ottoman landowners, mainly living outside Israel"
- Lived in mud hovels; large areas uncultivated
- Rashid Khalidi: fellahin became aware of Zionist danger earlier than commonly thought
- Land purchases and transfers triggered "serious peasant-led rebellions"
- Beshara Doumani's "Rediscovering Palestine": shows peasant agency through court records

## Central Asia

### Disruption of Kazakh Nomadic Life
- Russian fort-building limited territories used for herding
- 1890s onward: Russian settler colonization of fertile northern/eastern Kazakhstan
- Stolypin Reforms (1906-12): 500,000+ Russian farms, seizing grazing lands and blocking ancient migration routes
- 1890s famine: killed up to 100,000 in Semirechye

### Cotton and Forced Labor
- Turkestan became majority source of Russian Empire's cotton
- "Mississippiazation" of the region
- Settlers used native slave labor to build homesteads

### 1916 Uprising
- Imperial authorities conscripted previously exempt native population into labor battalions
- Kazakhs "driven to desperation by loss of lands and ruthlessness of wartime administration"
- Crushed "with violence not yet seen in the Russian Empire"
