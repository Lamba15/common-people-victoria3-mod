## Egypt-Specific History

### The Corvee System and Fellahin Life

- Fellahin were forced into corvee labor gangs for public works, irrigation, canal construction
- By 1865-1868, the government imposed a 70% increase in land taxes
- By early 1870s, one-third of rural Egypt was completely landless
- Corvee officially abolished after 1882 British takeover (but enforcement was slow)
- Muhammad Ali dictated annually what and how much peasants could grow
- Government monopolized purchase and sale of peasant goods -- the farmer had NO market freedom

### Conscription Horror

- Muhammad Ali's 1822 conscription enrolled 130,000 men, mostly fellahin
- 1824 revolt in the south showed how hated military service was
- Peasants fled villages to avoid conscription, sometimes as far as Syria
- **Men ripped out their own front teeth to avoid service** -- strong front teeth were needed to reload rifles. This is a mod event.
- Wives and families were no longer allowed to follow armies -- families torn apart by policy

### The Cotton Boom and Bust (1861-1865)

- American Civil War created massive demand for Egyptian cotton
- Exports surged from 600,000 cantars (1861) to 1.3 million (1863)
- Cotton became 93% of Egypt's export revenue
- The boom TRIPLED the rural slave population: 39,762 (1848) to 144,592 (1868)
- When the American Civil War ended, British traders abandoned Egyptian cotton for cheaper American supply
- Egypt went bankrupt in 1876
- Medium landholders (village headmen) benefited most -- small holders and large estates saw negligible gains

### The Suez Canal (1859-1869) -- Forced Labor

- Estimated 20,000 to 120,000 workers died during construction
- Causes of death: dysentery, smallpox, tuberculosis, hepatitis, cholera
- In 1865, there weren't enough men to bury the corpses
- Workers were filtered at Al-Zaqaziq: weak ones rejected, strong ones marched four days on foot while tied with ropes
- Given only a bottle of water and bread for the march
- Two-tier system: European banks/bakeries/bars on one side, worker misery on the other
- **This is a mod event chain.** If the player builds the Suez Canal, common people are conscripted to die building it.

### Egyptian Women (19th Century)

- Rural women worked alongside men in fields
- Urban working-class women: weavers, street vendors, midwives, washerwomen, domestic servants
- By late 19th century, female role in urban production CONTRACTED -- women pushed to casual service work
- Women in "improper" occupations (dancing, performing, coffeehouse serving) were monitored by police, arrested, or forcibly relocated
- Cairo wage laborers' purchasing power DECLINED from 1800 to 1907

### Egyptian Feminism

- **Huda Sha'arawi** (1879-1947): Egypt's first feminist
- 1909: Created first philanthropic society run by Egyptian women
- 1914: Founded Intellectual Association of Egyptian Women
- 1923: Founded Egyptian Feminist Union -- suffrage, education, legal reform
- 1923: Iconic veil removal at Cairo train station returning from Rome conference
- First phase of feminism (late 1800s to 1919): veiling, seclusion, marriage, divorce
- Middle/upper-class women led, but the issues affected ALL women

### The Urabi Revolt (1882)

- Emerged from economic hardship among peasant-farmers
- Anger at taxes going to wealthy Europeans and Turks
- Common people regarded Ahmad Urabi as a hero despite state media calling him a traitor
- Riots: homes looted, parts of Cairo burned, ~50 Europeans and ~250 Egyptians killed
- Laid foundation for mass politics in Egypt
- **This is a revolution event.** Common people characters should react to this.

### British Occupation (1882-1914)

- "Veiled protectorate" -- British Consul-General had ultimate power, Khedive was nominal ruler
- Lord Cromer (1883-1907): imposed laissez-faire policies benefiting British investors
- Cotton production emphasized for British mills -- enriched landowners, impoverished peasants
- **Purchasing power of Cairo wage laborers declined from 1800 to 1907**
- 13-hour days in cotton gins, 17-hour days in some sectors
- Modernization (railroads, schools, hospitals) was real BUT profits went to Cairo businessmen and London investors

### Muhammad Abduh and Islamic Modernism

- (1849-1905): Led movement to revitalize Islam for the modern world
- Three-part program: free Islamic thought from blind imitation, reform Arabic literature, increase political participation
- At al-Azhar University: introduced curriculum combining religion with science, math, philosophy
- Replaced memorization with critical thinking
- Rejected literal Quranic interpretation in favor of equity and common sense
- **This is a character.** An imam who represents reform Islam. Conflicts with traditionalist clergy.

## Factory Life (Britain and Egypt)

### Working Conditions

- 12-16 hour shifts
- No safety measures, no compensation for injury
- Factories were hot, poorly ventilated, filled with dust and machine oil
- Cotton mills: respiratory diseases from cotton dust
- Iron works: burns, crushing injuries
- Textile mills: fingers, arms, hair caught in machinery
- **Factory fines**: mills changed clocks to catch workers arriving "late" and fine them
- Fines for: talking, looking out the window, arriving late, leaving station

### Wages

- Male workers: ~15 shillings/week (75p)
- Women: ~7 shillings/week (35p)
- Children: ~3 shillings/week (15p)
- In Egypt: industrial workers earned barely enough to survive on 13-hour days
- Countryside cotton gin workers faced 17-hour days with even lower wages

### Housing

- Families in as small a space as possible
- 1830s Liverpool: one-third of population in house cellars with earth floors, no ventilation, 16 people per room
- "Rookeries": overcrowded, scantily equipped, poorly ventilated, unhygienic
- Open sewers overflowed through slums
- "Doss houses": cheap Victorian hotels for those without permanent residence

### Women in Factories

- Pregnant women worked "up to the very day, some up to the very hour" of delivery
- Most returned to work a fortnight after giving birth
- Mothers breastfed at breakfast, noon, and evening; babies left with childminders during 12-hour shifts
- **Babies kept quiet with opium.** Laudanum mixed with sugar and water. "Godfrey's Cordial" (laudanum + treacle) called "Mother's Friend." Children became too drugged to eat, grew malnourished, some died of starvation while others became sickly and stunted.
- By 1873, over 26% of cotton mill workers were married women, three-quarters of childbearing age

## Child Labor

### Ages and Scale

- Children as young as 4 worked in factories and mines
- By 1830, children comprised 50% of Great Britain's factory workforce
- 12-hour night shifts common for children
- Earned 10-20% of adult male wages (sometimes one-eighth)

### Conditions

- Coal mines: children ages 5+ stripped mostly naked, chained to carts pulling coal
- Match factories: phosphorous poisoning caused teeth to rot and glow ("phossy jaw") and death
- Cotton mills: respiratory damage, crushed limbs in machinery
- Rickets from indoor work without sunlight
- **Robert Blincoe**: orphaned at 7, sent to cotton mill, endured physical punishments, permanent health damage. His 1828 memoir led to the 1833 Factory Act.

### Mortality

- In the 1840s, about 1 in 6 infants died before age 1
- About 1 in 3 children died before age 5
- Only 50% of children survived to age 10 in industrial cities
- Agricultural areas: mortality below 100 per 1,000 births
- Some urban industrial areas: exceeded 200 per 1,000

### Factory Acts (Gradual Reform)

- **1833**: Prohibited workers under 9; 9-12 year olds limited to 8 hours; 13-17 year olds to 12 hours. First factory inspectors (four total).
- **1844**: Extended to women; 12-hour max; children 6.5 hours with 3 hours school
- **1847**: Ten Hours Act -- women and young people (13-18) in textile mills limited to 10 hours

## Serfdom and Emancipation (Russia 1861)

### The Emancipation

- 23+ million serfs freed
- Gained rights to marry without consent, own property, own businesses
- Peasants could "purchase" land from landlords

### The Redemption Payment Trap

- Government advanced 75% of land cost to landlords as bonds
- Peasants repaid the government with interest over **49 years** (not cancelled until 1907)
- Payments so high peasants had to sell ALL produced grain just to pay -- nothing left for survival
- Most peasants received LESS land than they had tilled before emancipation
- Payments often exceeded the rental cost of allotments
- **Debt passed to children.** Generational trap.

### What Actually Happened to Freed Serfs

- Communes replaced landlords as controllers -- peasants couldn't sell land or leave without commune permission
- Communes rarely granted permission, fearing financial burden on remaining members
- Former household serfs got NO land -- worst off of all
- A well-to-do peasant class eventually emerged, but most remained poor and land-hungry
- Population growth (1861-1905) made survival increasingly difficult
- **Freedom was real but hollow.** Legal liberty without economic liberty.

## War and Common People

### Crimean War (1853-1856)

- Egypt sent 40,000 soldiers under Ottoman command
- Egyptians "fought better and were more disciplined than the regular Ottoman army" (French observers)
- One Egyptian ship (Miftah-i cihad) sank with ~1,000 aboard; only 130 survived
- References to "families of the martyrs" indicate profound impact on families left behind

### "Soldier's Heart" / PTSD

- 19th century term for what we call PTSD
- Symptoms: fatigue, weakness, shortness of breath, rapid heartbeat, sweating, chest pain from mild exertion
- Also called "nostalgia" -- homesickness so severe soldiers became listless, emaciated, sometimes died
- Thousands of Union soldiers (US Civil War) developed "nostalgia" -- second most common diagnosis
- **19th century had no psychiatric understanding.** Men with war trauma were thought to have character flaws.

### Conscription Burden

- Ottoman exemption system (bedel) meant the rich avoided service -- peasants bore the load
- When men were conscripted, farms lost their primary labor
- Families depended on women and children to maintain the farm
- If the soldier died, the family lost both their worker and their future

## Named Individuals (For Character Inspiration)

### Robert Blincoe (c. 1792-1860)
Orphan, factory child at age 7. His memoir exposed child labor and changed law.

### The Tolpuddle Martyrs (1834)
Six farm laborers: George Loveless, James Loveless, Thomas Standfield, John Standfield, James Brine, James Hammett. Formed a union, transported to Australia, sparked the trade union movement. Wages had been cut from 9 to 7 shillings when basic survival cost 13 shillings.

### Hannah Cullwick (c. 1833-?)
Domestic servant whose diaries survive. Entered service at age 8. Intimate detail of working-class women's daily lives.

### Taha Hussein (1889-1973)
Blind Egyptian scholar. Autobiography "An Egyptian Childhood" describes rural Upper Egypt village life. Blinded at age 2 through malpractice, attended al-Azhar University.

### Huda Sha'arawi (1879-1947)
Egypt's first feminist. Founded Egyptian Feminist Union. Iconic veil removal in 1923.

### Ahmad Urabi (1841-1911)
Egyptian army officer from fellahin background. Led the 1882 revolt against the Khedive and foreign influence. Common people's hero.

## Event Ideas From This Research

### Egyptian-Specific Events
- **Tooth extraction**: A man rips out his teeth to avoid conscription. His wife watches.
- **Cotton boom**: Layla's harvest is worth triple this year. She buys a dress. Next year, the Americans end their war and the price collapses.
- **Suez Canal conscription**: Layla's husband is taken to dig. Conditions are lethal. Does he come back?
- **British occupation**: A foreign official inspects the village. He doesn't speak Arabic. He writes things in a ledger.
- **Urabi revolt**: The village takes sides. Layla's neighbor joins the rebels. The British come.
- **Veil removal**: A young woman in Cairo removes her veil after hearing about Sha'arawi. Her family reacts.
- **Al-Azhar reform**: The village imam returns from Cairo with new ideas. The old imam is furious.

### Factory Events
- **The drugged baby**: A mother gives her infant laudanum so it will sleep while she works. The baby stops eating.
- **Phossy jaw**: A match factory worker's teeth begin to glow in the dark. She is dying of phosphorous poisoning.
- **The factory fine**: A worker arrives 2 minutes late. The mill changed the clock. He loses a day's wages.
- **The factory inspector**: The first government inspector arrives. The foreman hides the children.

### Child Events
- **First day of school**: A child who has worked since age 5 sits in a classroom for the first time. Cannot hold a pencil because her fingers are deformed from the loom.
- **The coal mine child**: A boy of 6, stripped naked, chained to a cart, pulls coal through a tunnel too small for adults.
- **Robert Blincoe event**: An orphan is sent to a mill. He is 7. His new life begins.

### War Events
- **The letter that doesn't come**: A family waits for a letter from the front. It doesn't come. It doesn't come the next week either.
- **Soldier's heart**: A man returns from war. He flinches at loud noises. He can't sleep. Nobody understands what's wrong with him. They think he's weak.
- **The ship that sank**: News reaches the village that a transport ship sank. 870 men drowned. Was he on it? Nobody knows yet.

### Serfdom/Emancipation Events
- **The redemption payment**: Freed serfs discover that freedom costs money. 49 years of payments. Their children will inherit the debt.
- **The commune trap**: A freed serf wants to leave for the city. The commune says no -- if he leaves, his share of the payment falls on the others.
- **Household serf**: A house servant is freed. He gets no land. He is free and has nothing. He sleeps in the street.
